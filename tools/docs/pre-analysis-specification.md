# Pre-analysis specification, fixed before the A2 draw

**Not a hypothesis pre-registration**, and not required by anyone: RQ1 and RQ2 are measurement
questions, and [P024](../sources/P024-feuerriegel-2026-guide-llm-checklist.md)'s 14 items contain no
such requirement (PDF p. 6: it "is not intended to prescribe specific modelling choices"). The
warrant is narrower and it is the chair's own — P024, PDF p. 23, names the risk that "both data
generation and analysis could be gamed through careful prompting and parameter tuning" and says
addressing it "may need more formalized standards for documentation, including the use of
preregistrations." This document exists so the report the checklist *does* require is truthful.

**An authority index, not a fourth plan.** Every section either *fixes* something that is fixed
nowhere else, or *points* at where it is already fixed and adds nothing. Any sentence that restates
a decision another document owns has failed the test and should be deleted — restatement is how this
repository once lost a caveat through twenty locations. A reviewer's first cut of this document ran
to fifteen sections and sixteen open questions; it was rejected on exactly this ground, as "the
failure mode wearing the remedy's clothes".

## 3 — RQ3: the stability estimand, and what may not be said

*Revised 2026-09-05. The previous §3 defined D_c over presentation pairs for three conditions —
replicate, scaffolding, language. Two of the three are closed; what remains is the replicate
condition, now a sub-question in its own right.*

For each label dimension *d* (technology set at paragraph level; element set, evaluation map,
comparison target and subtype at relation level), each comparison unit *u* and each pair of
successful executions (*r* < *s*) of the identical request bytes: the indicator that *d*'s canonical
value at *u* differs between *r* and *s*, with "relation absent" as a value. Successful means
`technologies_present` or `off_topic`; a failed execution enters no pair and is counted in the
failure rate. The relation-level unit universe of a paragraph is the union of technologies over its
successful executions; a paragraph with an empty union has a technology-level change rate of zero
and **no relation-level score**. Per paragraph: the mean over units and pairs; across paragraphs:
the design-weighted mean over the paragraphs to which the level applies; uncertainty by article
resampling. Reported as **absolute change rates** per dimension and language, with the valid-pair
coverage and the count of paragraphs without pairs, and named as conditional on successful
executions.

**What may not be said.** The change rate is a property of the instrument under repetition, not a
validity claim and not a variance component of anything. The ordering across dimensions is
descriptive. No sentence in the results or discussion reads a difference between dimensions or
languages as evidence about language. Execution 1 is the primary prediction for every agreement
figure whatever its outcome; a majority vote is not computed, because it would evaluate a different
pipeline than the one Chapter 4 describes.

## 4d — Exploratory DSL estimate of frame-level prevalences (declared 2026-10-19; exploratory, author's and supervisor's decision)

> *Purpose.* The supervisor asked for a design-based supervised learning (DSL) estimate (Egami, Hinck, Stewart & Wei 2023) as an exploratory analysis. It corrects the model's aggregate prevalences in the frozen frame with the human-coded probability sample, under the sample's known inclusion probabilities; it is reported as an exploratory, descriptive analysis beside the registered displacement, never as a registered result, and it changes nothing about the registered run or the reference.
>
> *Estimand.* For every class c of the registered displacement table (a technology, an element, a frame–evaluation pair), the share of paragraphs of the **frozen eligible frame** (N = 618) on which c is present, with the DSL estimator
> θ̂_DSL(c) = (1/N) Σ_i [ ŷ_i(c) + (R_i/π_i)(y_i(c) − ŷ_i(c)) ],
> where ŷ_i(c) is the model's execution-one indicator on paragraph i, y_i(c) the reference indicator, R_i = 1 if i is in the registered A2 sample and π_i its registered inclusion probability (n_h/N_h). Reported beside it: the naive model prevalence over the frame (1/N) Σ ŷ_i, the design-weighted reference prevalence on the sample, and the registered displacement. Uncertainty: the 2.5–97.5 percentile interval over 2,000 resamples of entire frame articles drawn with replacement (seed 20260905), the sampled indicator travelling with its paragraph: a descriptive article-reweighting interval with no design-based coverage guarantee (it does not reproduce the stratified sampling variance and can be non-zero for a perfect predictor), as every interval of a registered figure in this thesis (the amendment below adds a design-based one to this exploratory estimate). A paragraph whose execution ended without a valid record contributes ŷ = 0, as the registered scoring treats failures, with the count reported. Cells whose reference support in the sample is below s = 5 print the state, not a value.
>
> *Inputs and their provenance.* Model predictions on every frame paragraph are required: (i) the registered run `a2_sonnet_20261013` (execution one) for the 120 sampled paragraphs; (ii) a **frame-completion run** of the registered instrument v1.0 (model, prompt, tool configuration, decoding, cache point unchanged; one execution; certified gate; journalled) on the 443 frame paragraphs that are neither in the A2 sample nor in the A3 census, listed in `labels/A2_frame_completion_manifest.json` (kind `frame_completion_manifest`, identity and metadata from the frozen frame, A2 and A3 excluded by digest, text-free, committed before the run) under the analysis status **`exploratory frame completion`**; the runner accepts this kind only under v1.0, one execution, with a certified gate, and the evaluator refuses to score it (no reference exists for those paragraphs); (iii) the A3 run (execution one) for the 55 census paragraphs once it exists. **No estimate is computed before all three exist** (revised 2026-10-19 after a Codex round: the remainder of the frame outside A3 is defined by which articles the A2 draw left untouched, so it is not a population the registered inclusion probabilities describe; an earlier clause allowing a "domain" estimate is withdrawn). The estimator is the fixed-predictor generalised-difference form of Egami and colleagues' pseudo-outcome (the model's indicator is fixed, not cross-fitted); with this stratified allocation Σ_{A2} 1/π_i = N, so the Horvitz–Thompson and Hájek reference means coincide. Target: the prevalence the human coding instrument would record on the frame. The A3 run enters only after the evaluator's A3 evidence check (reference finalised and committed before its first call) and only if every run bound the registered request (model, prompt, tool configuration, codebook, decoding, retry policy). Implementation: `tools/pipeline/dsl.py`, writing `tools/runs/tables/dsl/` and the compact table for Chapter 6 with the label "exploratory DSL estimate".
>
> *Claim limits.* DSL requires the sampled paragraphs to be a probability sample of the frame (they are, by design) and corrects the aggregate only; it establishes nothing about individual labels, and the article-resampling interval is descriptive. No threshold, instrument, prompt or reference changes; the registered displacement remains the registered figure.
>
> *Amendment 2026-10-19 (after the ingest of [P100](../sources/P100-angelopoulos-2023-prediction-powered-inference.md), Angelopoulos et al. 2023; before any A3 prediction exists; revised the same day after one Codex round).* Beside the descriptive article-reweighting interval, `dsl.py` reports the **design-based standard error** of θ̂_DSL under the registered stratified draw. The first sum of the estimator is a census constant, so its design variance is that of the Horvitz–Thompson total of the residual e_i = y_i − ŷ_i on the sample, estimated per variance group g as (1 − f_g) · n_g/(n_g − 1) · Σ_{i∈g} (w_i e_i − v̄_g)², summed over groups and divided by N², with f_g = n_g/N_g for a group that is a single stratum (the exact stratified-SRSWOR estimator) and f_g = 0 for a pooled group. Groups: the strata of each language are walked in the order of the sample manifest's allocation list (sorted by language and outlet name) and a group closes once it holds two draws, so the three single-draw strata form two pooled groups (Berliner Morgenpost with DER SPIEGEL; STERN.de with Salzburger Nachrichten) and 16 groups result from the 18 strata; a pooled group keeps the with-replacement form without the finite-population factor, which overstates its variance in expectation (with the factor it can understate it, as the Codex counterexample of 2026-10-19 shows; test D19 confirms both on one enumerated design). A normal 95 % interval on this standard error is printed beside the descriptive interval, with the same standard error of the reference-only (Horvitz–Thompson) estimate as the classical comparison. This is a design-based analogue of the prediction-powered interval derived here for the stratified draw, not the published result for random samples: in that reading the first sum is the imputed mean and the second the design-weighted negative of the rectifier (Table 1 of P100 writes the measure of fit as θ minus the imputed mean and the rectifier as the mean of ŷ − y). Still exploratory; the predictor is fixed (execution noise is in neither interval); the normal approximation rests on 120 residuals, the reporting threshold counts reference positives and not non-zero residuals, and nominal 95 % coverage is not guaranteed. The convention of L24 for every registered figure (article-reweighting intervals; analytic standard errors as sensitivity only) is unchanged; this interval belongs to the exploratory estimate alone. Tests D13–D20 in `test_dsl.py`. *Status (2026-10-19).* Computed after the A3 run from the three runs; `paper/tables/tab_dsl.tex` and `tools/runs/tables/dsl/`; reported in Chapter 6 as the exploratory DSL estimate.

Nothing changes the codebook v1.0, the registered instrument or any reported figure.
>
> *Construct.* A **label-free instrument-sensitivity screen**: it locates where the instrument's labels move under meaning-preserving or structural perturbations. It is a triage signal for the coders, not proof of a definition gap; the gap reading is earned only by the validation below.
>
> *Material and request.* The 443 frame-completion paragraphs outside A2 and A3 (never human-coded; already sent to Bedrock EU; `sc.verify_frame_completion` before any call). Executions are interleaved in a seeded random order (seed 20261030) over four workers.
>
> *Variants (executions per variant over the 443 paragraphs).* **R** baseline × 3. **O1** frame blocks in reverse order, elements kept within their block × 2; **O2** technology list reversed (`general` first → last) × 2. **G** element labels replaced by neutral tokens ("Element 1" … "Element 17"), ids, frame names and definitions unchanged (an invariance adaptation of Halterman & Keith's Test VI, which they run with labels) × 2. **K1** the `general` definition plus the explicit per-statement reading ("Tick `general` whenever a bundle term — renewables, clean power, green energy, the energy transition — carries a statement of its own, even if the same paragraph also names individual technologies.") × 1; **K1c** the parallel sentence for `hydrogen` and `nuclear` (negative controls; coder α = 1.0) × 1; **K2** the `general` definition plus the explicit residual reading ("Tick `general` only when no individual technology is identifiable in the paragraph; otherwise record the statement under the named technology.") × 1. **E1/E2** planted gaps: the secondary rules (does-not-fire, boundary, boundary-against-F5a, recall note) removed from a seeded half of the eight elements that carry them (E1) and from the other half (E2) × 2 each. **Pa1/Pa2** all 34 definition texts (7 technologies, 17 fires-when texts, 10 secondary rules) replaced by paraphrase set 1 of Kimi K3 (Pa1) or of Qwen3-235B (Pa2) × 2 each. **Pu** one unit at a time — the important units `general`, `hydrogen` (control), F2b, F2c, F3b, F5a, F6b (the documented gap, the lowest coder α, the codebook's named boundaries) — with four paraphrase sets each (Kimi s1, Qwen s1, Kimi s2, Qwen s2) × 1: 28 variants. **A2 subset** (unit-level validation): R × 2, Pa1, Pa2, O1 × 1 on the 120 A2 paragraphs. Priority under the budget cap, in this order: R, E, K, O, G, Pa, Pu, A2 subset.
>
> *Paraphrase protocol.* Generators: Kimi K3 (`global.moonshotai.kimi-k3`, the only profile the account can call; it receives codebook text only, and the few verbatim corpus quotes inside definitions are masked before sending and restored after) and Qwen3-235B (`qwen.qwen3-235b-a22b-2507-v1:0`, EU). Fixed instruction: same meaning exactly; add, remove, narrow or broaden no condition; resolve no ambiguity; keep ids, frame names, backtick spans, numbers and quotes verbatim; English; roughly the same length. Automatic checks: preserved tokens, length ratio 0.6–1.6. Blinded equivalence judging by two judges, Codex and a Claude judge, on shuffled original–paraphrase pairs: accepted only if both rate it meaning-equivalent and neither finds a resolved ambiguity; a rejected text is regenerated once and judged again; if rejected twice, the all-texts sets keep the original text for it and the one-at-a-time variant is dropped (both reported). The author's own check is not a precondition (the author asked to proceed without pausing); the accepted sets are reported with their rejection rates.
>

is an author convention and the text must say so. Printed folio unless a row says PDF page.
