\subsection{Evaluator Conventions}\label{app:evaluator_conventions}

Table~\ref{tab:conventions} lists the conventions the evaluator applies. Macro averages are taken over the classes $C^{+}$ with positive reference support in the population scored, pooled or one language; with design-weighted per-class scores $F_{1,c}^{w}$, the macro-F1 is
\begin{equation}
F_1^{\mathrm{macro}} = \frac{1}{|C^{+}|} \sum_{c \in C^{+}} F_{1,c}^{w}.
\label{eq:macrof1}
\end{equation}
For the registered model comparisons, intervals recompute the statistic $T$ on article resamples drawn with replacement. Article multiplicities $m_a^{(b)}$ multiply the paragraph design weights $w_i$, so that
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
T^{(b)} &= T\bigl(\{\,m_{a(i)}^{(b)}\, w_i\,\}\bigr), \qquad B = 2{,}000, \\
\mathrm{CI}_{95} &= \bigl[\,q_{0.025}(T^{(1)}, \dots, T^{(B)}),\; q_{0.975}(T^{(1)}, \dots, T^{(B)})\,\bigr],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:resample}
\end{equation}
where $a(i)$ is the article of paragraph $i$ and $q_p$ the $p$-quantile of the $B$ resampled values.

\subsection{Adjusted Prevalences: Estimator and Standard Error}\label{app:dsl_details}

The design-based supervised learning estimator \parencite{egami2023dsl} is used here with the model's first-execution indicator as a fixed prediction, in place of the cross-fitted outcome model of the original estimator. Treating the prediction as fixed assumes that it was generated independently of whether a paragraph was sampled. It estimates the share of the $N = 618$ eligible-frame paragraphs on which a class $c$ is present as
\begin{equation}
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{DSL}}(c) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \Bigl[\, \hat{y}_i(c) + \frac{S_i}{\pi_i}\,\bigl(y_i(c) - \hat{y}_i(c)\bigr) \Bigr],
\label{eq:dsl_estimator}
\end{equation}
with $\hat{y}_i$ the model's first-execution indicator, 0 after a failed execution, $y_i$ the reference indicator, $S_i = 1$ for the 120 sampled paragraphs and $S_i = 0$ otherwise, and $\pi_i$ their registered inclusion probability. The second term is the bias-correction term of \textcite{egami2023dsl}, a design-weighted counterpart of the rectifier in prediction-powered inference \parencite[p.~669]{angelopoulos2023ppi}. Because the eligible frame is a census for the model's indicator, the only design variance under that assumption is that of this term. Under the registered stratified draw its standard error is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}\bigl(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{DSL}}\bigr) = \frac{1}{N} \sqrt{\sum_{g} \bigl(1 - f_g\bigr) \frac{n_g}{n_g - 1} \sum_{i \in g} \bigl(v_i - \bar{v}_g\bigr)^2 }, \qquad v_i = w_i \bigl(y_i(c) - \hat{y}_i(c)\bigr),
\label{eq:dsl_se}
\end{equation}
with $w_i = 1/\pi_i$, $\bar{v}_g$ the mean of $v_i$ in variance group $g$, $f_g = n_g/N_g$ for a group that is a single stratum and $f_g = 0$ for a pooled group. The eighteen strata form sixteen groups: the three single-draw strata are pooled with a neighbouring stratum of their language in allocation order (Berliner Morgenpost with DER SPIEGEL; STERN.de with Salzburger Nachrichten). A pooled group keeps the with-replacement form, which overstates its variance in expectation; pooling strata carries no such guarantee. The design-based interval is the estimate $\pm 1.96\,\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}$, so a class's standard error is the half-width of its design interval in Table~\ref{tab:dsl} divided by 1.96 (for investment, F2b, 3.4 points). It is derived for this draw, treats the predictor as fixed and has no guaranteed nominal coverage. The predictions come from three runs of the unchanged instrument, and the run followed the sample status. The registered run of 13 October 2026 covers the 120 sampled paragraphs and the fresh-census run of 19 October its 55. One execution on the same day covers the other 443 eligible-frame paragraphs (479 calls, USD 2.76). First executions without a valid record number 5 of 120 (4.2\%), 8 of 55 (14.5\%) and 30 of 443 (6.8\%), and count as zero indicators. The assumption of a prediction generated independently of sampling is therefore not verified: run date and paragraph composition differ together, and the residual is estimated on the registered run alone. If failures were more frequent outside the sample for reasons of the run, not of the paragraphs, the adjusted shares would lean downward. A class without any sampled coder--model difference has a zero-width interval, a plug-in artefact that says nothing about unsampled paragraphs. Table~\ref{tab:dsl} prints every class that reaches the reporting threshold. Of the 35 frame--evaluation pairs that either side marks anywhere in the eligible frame, nineteen fall below it; on the evaluation sample alone, either side marks 31 pairs, of which 15 fall below it (Section~\ref{subsec:displacement_results}). These pairs and two elements are in the electronic table.

Under the first coding, the coded sample alone puts investment at 40.1\%, close to the adjusted 37.7\% (Table~\ref{tab:dsl}). The model's labels lower the standard error from 4.3 to 3.4 percentage points. For hydrogen and nuclear, no sampled paragraph shows a coder--model difference, so their design-based intervals have zero width, an artefact of the variance estimate rather than precision.

\subsection{Translated Input}\label{app:language}\label{subsec:language_results}

The comparison was declared on 19 October 2026 and amended on 5 November 2026 before any paragraph was translated. Translation cost USD 0.02; the extraction run \modelid{a2de_translated_sonnet_20261105} made 188 calls at USD 1.04. The detailed table is electronic (Appendix~\ref{app:electronic}).

Against the first coding, translated minus original is $-0.022$ [$-0.084$, $+0.032$] for first-execution element micro-F1. Future studies should report precision and recall per label dimension and language, on source text and against a reference whose stability they report.

\subsection{The Rendered Instrument}\label{app:instrument}

{\emergencystretch=3em The system prompt of the registered instrument v1.0, rendered from the frozen codebook (sha256 \texttt{1edb4601}\ldots), is reproduced verbatim from the pinned file (\modelid{system-prompt_v1.0.txt}, sha256 \texttt{138cade3}\ldots), as every run of that instrument sent it. The paragraph itself is the only user message. The tool configuration that forces the single extraction tool (\modelid{tool-config_v1.0.json}, sha256 \texttt{a1e82607}\ldots) is in the electronic appendix (Table~\ref{tab:electronic}). Three sources of its sixteen quoted illustrations are established. The phrase on weak wind conditions and the figure of 132 TWh follow calibration paragraph A011\_p02; article A011 is excluded from the eligible frame for that reason. The illustration of deployment growth follows calibration paragraph A010\_p15; article A010 was not excluded and has four paragraphs in the eligible frame, two of them in the evaluation sample. Both are among the 15 paragraphs that the sensitivity check of Section~\ref{subsec:agreement} removes; the first coding marks deployment growth on one of them and the model on neither, which does not measure the illustration's influence. The one German illustration paraphrases article A002, all of whose paragraphs belong to the calibration split. The origin of the other illustrations was not traced.\par}

The prompt is not a verbatim copy of the codebook file. The renderer keeps every technology, element and evaluation definition and the frame mapping word for word, and treats the eleven exclusion rules of the codebook as its decision record of 2 September 2026 prescribes (Table~\ref{tab:rule_concordance}). The coder worked with the coding sheets generated from the codebook, the codebook file and a hand-written one-page summary of version 1.0; which of these was open in a given sitting was not recorded. Model and coder therefore share the definitions, not every rule text. One difference is substantive: only the prompt tells the model to record \emph{mixed} on a single relation when several voices evaluate in opposite directions. The first coding marks an attributed speaker on 77 of its 178 rows (Table~\ref{tab:checks_4p}); no field records opposing voices, so the effect of this difference on the evaluation figures is unknown.
