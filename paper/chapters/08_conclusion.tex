This thesis asked how a multilingual LLM pipeline can be designed and validated for extracting technology frames from international renewable energy news. The question matters because firms, investors and agencies monitoring coverage can use frame shares only from a validated instrument. The answer rests on validation against one coder on a 120-paragraph probability sample, design-weighted to the 618-paragraph eligible frame of a keyword-prefiltered, purposive corpus.

On design (RQ1), one frozen codebook supplies common definitions for the model's prompt and output schema and the coder's sheets. Its exclusion rules reach them differently, so identical application is not established. Zero-shot coding reserves the coder's labels for validation. One stateless call codes a paragraph's technologies jointly, as one sentence may contrast two, and untranslated, as translation can change labels. A registry, a raw-response journal and a design-weighted evaluator make every prediction traceable to its configuration.

On agreement (RQ2), technology detection (micro-F1 0.935) and evaluations of shared frames (matched-frame accuracy 0.874) agree closely with the first coding. End-to-end evaluation F1 is 0.617. Comparison targets reach 0.794 matched-row accuracy, against 0.674 for a later constant guess of ``none''. In later contrasts, German technology detection is lower when failed executions count as misses but not clearly so among valid first executions, a selected subset. The languages also come from different outlets.

Frame elements agree less, with a micro-F1 of 0.643 against the first coding and no clear language difference. A later split gives precision 0.76 against recall 0.56, so the model marks fewer elements. In later checks on the 40 paragraphs re-coded before the model run, the model's element F1 is 0.571 against the first coding and 0.620 against the second. There, the recall deficit narrows against the second coding but persists in point estimate. The two codings agree at 0.656, one coder's agreement over time, not a ceiling or validity. Neither coding is established as correct, so the central finding is a recall deficit against one coder, its size depending on the reference reading.

On stability (RQ3), identical repeated executions give similar scores but not identical outputs. Element micro-F1 ranges from 0.632 to 0.648. Yet some label of a paragraph changes between two successful executions at a design-weighted rate of 0.150.

On aggregate prevalences (RQ4), 11 of 15 reported element shares lie below the first coding's in point estimate, five with pointwise intervals excluding zero. Against the second coding, made mostly after the results, an exploratory sensitivity gives nine lower and six higher, two of the latter with intervals above zero. Only investment lies below both codings with intervals excluding zero, by 18.3 and 13.6 percentage points. A later design-based adjustment raises investment's share under either coding's convention but does not correct reference error. Sample size sets the limit: classes with fewer than five supporting reference paragraphs stay suppressed, and intervals 5.6 to 17.0 points wide make small displacements indistinguishable from zero.

The contribution is a tested combination, not a new model or method. It links label agreement, output variation, the coder's stability and share displacement, and, in later analyses, the reference dependence of adjusted shares. Once validated per language on the material at hand, such a pipeline can report technologies and the evaluations of confirmed frames. Its element and frame shares need their own check against a human-coded probability sample, with a reference secured before adjustment.

With one coder who had more context than the model, the deficit's sources cannot be apportioned. A later Opus~5 run shows that the setup does not force a deficit. Neither a second coder nor re-reading disagreements establishes validity alone. The next test is whether a narrower codebook, applied by two independent coders to the model's paragraph-only input, raises agreement between the coders and with the model on fresh paragraphs.

Other monitoring projects that define their own frames and revalidate can reuse the validation workflow.
