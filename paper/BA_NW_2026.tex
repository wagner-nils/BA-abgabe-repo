\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Firms and agencies monitoring energy-technology news could use language-model frame shares, but accuracy-validated labels can bias them. No reviewed study validates one such instrument at frame-element level, per language and on shares. Pre-specified rules compare a frozen-codebook pipeline with one coder on 120 German and English paragraphs, probability-sampled from a keyword-prefiltered, purposive corpus. Against the first coding, technology detection (micro-F1 0.935) and shared-frame evaluations (accuracy 0.874) agree closely, frame elements less (micro-F1 0.643). The model marks fewer elements. Most shares lie lower, investment's by 18.3 percentage points. Against 40 paragraphs re-coded before the results, later checks find this recall deficit smaller but persisting in point estimate. In exploratory checks against the full, mostly post-result re-code, investment's share differs by $-13.6$ [$-20.4$, $-7.6$] points. Neither coding is established as correct. Projects with their own frames can reuse the validation workflow. Shares need a human-coded probability sample and a defended coding convention.
\end{abstract}

\section{Electronic Appendix}\label{app:electronic}
The electronic appendix is the submission repository, a copy of the project repository at the commit named below without its version history. It holds the material behind every figure of this thesis and the tables not printed in Appendix~A. Table~\ref{tab:electronic} maps each retained claim to its files; paths are relative to the repository root, and the README describes the set-up, the pipeline and the reproduction check.

The files support two levels of reproduction. Every reported figure can be recomputed from the committed run records, labels and manifests without a model call; most generators re-render and compare their floats in a \texttt{-{}-check} mode. Re-running an extraction needs the article text, which is not in the repository, and access to Amazon Bedrock, and it may return different outputs, because the provider offers no seed.

\begin{description}
\item[Commit] {\small\texttt{0b77ebdc831508fe8c5cd4ab1b517899841c4643}} of the private project repository. The submitted PDF is built from this state; only this identifier was added afterwards.
\item[Withheld] Article and paragraph text, raw model responses, private journals and coding sheets, the stored translations, credentials. The texts are withheld as a precaution: the basis for storing, externally processing and redistributing the hand-collected articles was not clarified. The coding interface, a browser tool built outside the repository, is not archived in any build; its behaviour is documented by its written specification only.
\end{description}

This is the overview of tools used (\foreignlanguage{ngerman}{Übersicht verwendeter Hilfsmittel}) to which the statutory declaration at the end of this thesis refers. It names all generative models used for this thesis by product name and version, and states how, to what extent and for what purpose each was used. Generated wording is not marked passage by passage in the text. Its origin is declared here, and the author's statement after the table says how adopted passages were checked. Part~A lists the models that served as research instruments in the extraction pipeline; their configuration, prompts, data handling and validation are reported in the GUIDE-LLM checklist (\guideref) and in Chapters~\ref{sec:system_design} to~\ref{sec:results}. Part~B lists the AI assistants used in producing the thesis text, the code and the corpus; the author's own statement follows the table.

\textbf{Statement of extent by the author.} I wrote the basis of the thesis text largely myself, partly as full drafts and partly as bullet points. In several iterations, the assistants in part~B shortened passages, formulated bullet points as prose and helped me explore lines of argument. The final text was written almost exclusively by me and Claude. I checked every adopted passage by proofreading it directly, had GPT-6 Astra cross-check it, and read revised versions as comparison documents showing removed text in red and new text in blue.

\textbf{How, why and with what experience.} I used the assistants to brainstorm the structure of the thesis and its methods, to formulate and check text, to implement changes and correct errors, to analyse parts of the thesis, to aggregate information from the literature research and to organise it in an LLM-maintained wiki. I chose them because they let me check results quickly and rebuild the thesis quickly when results came in. Keeping information consistent was difficult despite the wiki, small tasks sometimes used many tokens, and the generated wording was at times too mechanical and hard to read.
